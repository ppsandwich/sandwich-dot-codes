# Screen alignment review

Scope: all 72 A/B/C screenshots across the 24 selected directions, plus descriptions for all 12 apps. Logos and original taglines are retained. Earlier original-direction screenshots are labelled as historical references.

The detailed [pedagogical outcomes](../pedagogical-outcomes.md) set the wider purpose. Each screen below demonstrates one assessable task within that purpose. A blank response field is intentional: the learner supplies the reasoning. The screenshots are design concepts, not functional app implementations or validated learning interventions.

Assessment should consider accuracy, reasoning and independence. Record whether the student used prompts, audio or partner support. Use an equivalent unfamiliar task to check transfer; do not infer mastery from clicks or completion.

All 72 revised screens were visually reviewed. Thirty-three received a further correction and were reviewed again, covering circuit symbols, measurement scales, map instructions, recording states and prompts that could supply the learner’s answer. See the [per-screen review record](visual-review.json) and [gallery checks](gallery-qa.json). All 72 standalone logos remain unchanged.

## Classroom Words

Teachers select the sound–letter relationships already taught. Students blend and segment words, then read controlled decodable text using that sequence. Teachers listen to independent attempts, note the support needed and check transfer to unfamiliar words.

**Primary outcome:** Read an unfamiliar word made from taught sound–letter relationships, blending independently before using audio support.

**Lesson placement:** guided practice immediately after explicit phonics teaching, a short literacy rotation or targeted consolidation with a teacher.

### Direction 3: Sound station — A, B and C

**Finding:** Audio controls and completed word-building did not distinguish independent decoding from copying a model.

**Revised task:** Blend a printed word before hearing a model.

**Evidence to assess:** Teacher listens for correct blending of pin, records prompts and checks a different word afterwards.

[A](../classroom-words/03-sound-station/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../classroom-words/03-sound-station/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../classroom-words/03-sound-station/c/screenshot.png)

### Direction 5: Sound circles — A, B and C

**Finding:** Matching tap to an identical written choice could be completed without decoding.

**Revised task:** Blend a word, then apply the same correspondences in a short sentence.

**Evidence to assess:** Teacher hears the word and sentence, notes accuracy and support, then checks an unfamiliar word.

[A](../classroom-words/05-sound-circles/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../classroom-words/05-sound-circles/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../classroom-words/05-sound-circles/c/screenshot.png)

**Transfer check:** Read different words on paper and spell a dictated word using the same correspondences.

## Think in Parts

Students construct an explanation using drawing, voice, animation and interactive diagrams. Structured challenges require them to show a relationship, predict what happens when one part changes and revise after feedback. Teachers assess the accuracy and reasoning in the explanation.

**Primary outcome:** Explain a causal relationship with a consistent diagram and narration, then justify a prediction when one component changes.

**Lesson placement:** an initial diagnostic task, consolidation after an investigation or an assessed explanation near the end of a unit.

### Direction 4: Voice and ink — A, B and C

**Finding:** A finished explanation and labels replaced the student’s causal reasoning.

**Revised task:** Explain a circuit relationship and predict the effect of a change.

**Evidence to assess:** Narration links the complete conducting path to the lamp and justifies a prediction about opening the switch.

[A](../think-in-parts/04-voice-and-ink/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../think-in-parts/04-voice-and-ink/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../think-in-parts/04-voice-and-ink/c/screenshot.png)

### Direction 1: Paper mechanism — A, B and C

**Finding:** A labelled diagram and supplied paragraph did not require an explanation from the student.

**Revised task:** Predict how changing a lever affects the effort needed and explain why.

**Evidence to assess:** Student connects relative lever-arm lengths to effort, with a consistent diagram and explanation.

[A](../think-in-parts/01-paper-mechanism/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../think-in-parts/01-paper-mechanism/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../think-in-parts/01-paper-mechanism/c/screenshot.png)

**Transfer check:** Explain a related example without replaying the finished presentation.

## Town Together

Students investigate a public issue, compare proposals and stakeholder evidence, and calculate costs within a defined budget. In a simulated council or parliamentary process, they justify a recommendation, acknowledge a trade-off and respond to an objection. Each scenario identifies the decision-making body and its responsibilities.

**Primary outcome:** Recommend a feasible proposal using evidence from competing perspectives, accurate budget reasoning and an explicit trade-off.

**Lesson placement:** the application stage of a civics unit, after teaching the relevant decision-making body.

### Direction 1: Council table — A, B and C

**Finding:** The proposals showed benefits without requiring a budget calculation or reasoned recommendation.

**Revised task:** Choose a proposal within a budget and justify the trade-off.

**Evidence to assess:** Recommendation cites two stakeholder needs, gives correct remaining funds and acknowledges what is forgone.

[A](../town-together/01-council-table/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../town-together/01-council-table/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../town-together/01-council-table/c/screenshot.png)

### Direction 4: People’s assembly — A, B and C

**Finding:** A unanimous positive scenario concealed competing needs and the limits of a funding decision.

**Revised task:** Weigh access against service costs in a council decision.

**Evidence to assess:** Student uses contrasting stakeholder evidence and project costs to justify a trial or an alternative.

[A](../town-together/04-people-s-assembly/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../town-together/04-people-s-assembly/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../town-together/04-people-s-assembly/c/screenshot.png)

**Transfer check:** Write an individual recommendation for a different community issue and identify the responsible institution.

## Wild Notebook

Students investigate biodiversity, weather, water and habitats through school-ground fieldwork. They record observations using agreed methods, compare data and distinguish what they observed from what they infer. Classroom discussion tests explanations against the evidence and identifies uncertainty or further observations needed.

**Primary outcome:** Make a comparison supported by systematically collected observations and state a limitation of the evidence.

**Lesson placement:** fieldwork preparation, outdoor data collection and subsequent discussion, or a repeated investigation across a term.

### Direction 1: Field journal — A, B and C

**Finding:** The observation lacked a sampling method and did not separate identification uncertainty from evidence.

**Revised task:** Make a field record another group can interpret.

**Evidence to assess:** Record includes place, duration, area, observation and a separately marked tentative inference.

[A](../wild-notebook/01-field-journal/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../wild-notebook/01-field-journal/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../wild-notebook/01-field-journal/c/screenshot.png)

### Direction 4: Habitat layers — A, B and C

**Finding:** Unequal or unspecified sampling and inconsistent categories could support an invalid biodiversity conclusion.

**Revised task:** Compare samples fairly and limit a conclusion to the evidence.

**Evidence to assess:** Student cites counts from both sites, distinguishes abundance from variety and states a sampling limitation.

[A](../wild-notebook/04-habitat-layers/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../wild-notebook/04-habitat-layers/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../wild-notebook/04-habitat-layers/c/screenshot.png)

**Transfer check:** Repeat sampling at another location or time using the same method and reconsider the explanation.

## TinkerTest

Students identify a testable question, choose an independent variable, hold relevant conditions constant and make a prediction. They run a virtual investigation, interpret results and revise a conclusion using numerical evidence. Teachers help students distinguish simulation results from claims requiring physical investigation.

**Primary outcome:** Identify what changes, what is measured and what stays constant; use results to support or revise a prediction.

**Lesson placement:** investigation rehearsal before practical work, exploration when equipment is unavailable or extension after practical work.

### Direction 5: Evidence engine — A, B and C

**Finding:** Supplied predictions and conclusions removed the investigation’s reasoning steps.

**Revised task:** Use a controlled simulation to test a prediction about pendulum length.

**Evidence to assess:** Student identifies the variable and controls, cites two data points and explains whether results support the prediction.

[A](../tinkertest/05-evidence-engine/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../tinkertest/05-evidence-engine/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../tinkertest/05-evidence-engine/c/screenshot.png)

### Direction 1: Workshop lab — A, B and C

**Finding:** Ramp geometry and measurement scale were inconsistent, and the result explanation was supplied.

**Revised task:** Relate ramp height to measured travel distance while controlling other conditions.

**Evidence to assess:** Student names height as the changed variable and distance as the measurement, and justifies a pattern using results.

[A](../tinkertest/01-workshop-lab/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../tinkertest/01-workshop-lab/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../tinkertest/01-workshop-lab/c/screenshot.png)

**Transfer check:** Design a physical investigation of a related question and identify limitations of the virtual model.

## Number Kitchen

Students solve cooking challenges involving quantities, equal sharing, fractions, measurement and money. Teachers choose one mathematical focus and level of difficulty. Students predict an amount, represent their strategy and check the result, with optional hands-on measuring and sharing to test transfer.

**Primary outcome:** Find an unknown quantity in a recipe or measuring task, represent a valid strategy and check that the result meets the target.

**Lesson placement:** guided application after explicit teaching, a mathematics rotation or a bridge between physical materials and symbolic work.

### Direction 2: Recipe notebook — A, B and C

**Finding:** The completed recipe provided the quantities the child was meant to calculate.

**Revised task:** Scale every ingredient by the same factor.

**Evidence to assess:** Student finds quantities for four portions, explains doubling and checks with physical counters.

[A](../number-kitchen/02-recipe-notebook/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../number-kitchen/02-recipe-notebook/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../number-kitchen/02-recipe-notebook/c/screenshot.png)

### Direction 4: Measure lab — A, B and C

**Finding:** The extra amount was already displayed, eliminating the missing-quantity problem.

**Revised task:** Find the missing volume needed to reach a target.

**Evidence to assess:** Student calculates the difference with units, predicts before pouring and checks the final measurement.

[A](../number-kitchen/04-measure-lab/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../number-kitchen/04-measure-lab/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../number-kitchen/04-measure-lab/c/screenshot.png)

**Transfer check:** Solve a similar problem with real measuring equipment or counters, without the app scaffold.

## StoryChirps

Students arrange picture sequences and record a story for a listener. Teacher prompts focus on sequencing, description or audience clarity. Students listen back, identify one point to improve and record a revised retelling. Different interpretations are welcome, with recordings retained in a private classroom collection.

**Primary outcome:** Tell a connected oral story, identify a point a listener may not understand and improve that point in a second recording.

**Lesson placement:** oral-language groups, preparation for writing, responses to shared texts or short formative assessments.

### Direction 4: Little story stage — A, B and C

**Finding:** The screen offered recording without a specific listening or revision purpose.

**Revised task:** Improve the clarity of a spoken retelling for a listener.

**Evidence to assess:** Two recordings and a listener’s response show whether who, where and event sequence became clearer.

[A](../storychirps/04-little-story-stage/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../storychirps/04-little-story-stage/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../storychirps/04-little-story-stage/c/screenshot.png)

### Direction 2: Story nest — A, B and C

**Finding:** Picture ordering alone did not reveal whether the student could produce a connected oral narrative.

**Revised task:** Use sequence language and revise an unclear connection in an oral story.

**Evidence to assess:** Student connects the picture events in speech and improves one transition after listening or partner feedback.

[A](../storychirps/02-story-nest/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../storychirps/02-story-nest/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../storychirps/02-story-nest/c/screenshot.png)

**Transfer check:** Retell a new picture sequence to a partner and check what the listener understood.

## Plot a Place

Students make maps of familiar and imaginary places using symbols, a key, directions and age-appropriate scale. They write navigation challenges, test a partner’s route and revise unclear instructions or map features. They also explain how the location of a feature serves a particular need.

**Primary outcome:** Create or interpret a consistent map, give a route another person can follow and justify one placement decision.

**Lesson placement:** after a local walk, within a mapping sequence or as a design-and-justify task.

### Direction 4: Classroom compass — A, B and C

**Finding:** The design suggested a layout but did not require an authored navigation task or a peer test.

**Revised task:** Write and test a route using a consistent classroom map.

**Evidence to assess:** A partner reaches the intended landmark using the student’s route; the student revises an unclear step.

[A](../plot-a-place/04-classroom-compass/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../plot-a-place/04-classroom-compass/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../plot-a-place/04-classroom-compass/c/screenshot.png)

### Direction 2: Little town planner — A, B and C

**Finding:** The neighbourhood was decorative and did not elicit spatial reasoning from the student.

**Revised task:** Justify a map placement using distance and a community need.

**Evidence to assess:** Student uses the key and scale to compare route distances and explains how a chosen location meets a need.

[A](../plot-a-place/02-little-town-planner/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../plot-a-place/02-little-town-planner/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../plot-a-place/02-little-town-planner/c/screenshot.png)

**Transfer check:** Use a paper map during a school walk, then revise it after testing the route.

## Clue Canopy

Students investigate illustrated stories by selecting relevant textual clues and explaining what those clues suggest. They distinguish observation from inference, consider another plausible explanation and revise a prediction when new evidence appears. Speech, drawing and writing reveal reasoning without requiring a single predetermined interpretation.

**Primary outcome:** State a plausible inference, select a relevant text detail and explain the connection while acknowledging uncertainty.

**Lesson placement:** guided reading, comprehension mini-lessons, paired discussion or diagnostic reasoning checks.

### Direction 2: Evidence board — A, B and C

**Finding:** A leading question and selected clues encouraged accusation rather than evidence-based inference.

**Revised task:** Infer a possible event and explain how a text clue supports it.

**Evidence to assess:** Student states a plausible claim, selects relevant words and explains a connection without asserting unsupported guilt.

[A](../clue-canopy/02-evidence-board/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../clue-canopy/02-evidence-board/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../clue-canopy/02-evidence-board/c/screenshot.png)

### Direction 4: Margin detective — A, B and C

**Finding:** The supplied explanation and illustrative answer reduced the need to interpret the text.

**Revised task:** Distinguish what a text states from a reasonable inference.

**Evidence to assess:** Student quotes a relevant clue, explains its implication and recognises that more than one explanation may fit.

[A](../clue-canopy/04-margin-detective/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../clue-canopy/04-margin-detective/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../clue-canopy/04-margin-detective/c/screenshot.png)

**Transfer check:** Make and defend an inference from a fresh short passage without suggested answers.

## SwitchSide Stories

Through teacher-guided illustrated scenarios, students explore disagreements, joining games and working together. They identify different needs, consider possible perspectives and compare likely consequences of more than one response. Students rehearse a constructive response and reflect, with room for several reasonable answers and adult support.

**Primary outcome:** Identify two possible perspectives, compare the likely effects of two responses and explain a constructive choice.

**Lesson placement:** teacher-led discussion, a planned relationships lesson or preparation for cooperative work.

### Direction 2: Story seesaw — A, B and C

**Finding:** Response options alone did not ask students to consider both needs or likely consequences.

**Revised task:** Consider two needs and compare responses to a shared-material disagreement.

**Evidence to assess:** Student explains likely effects on both children and rehearses a response, without being graded on a prescribed emotion.

[A](../switchside-stories/02-story-seesaw/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../switchside-stories/02-story-seesaw/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../switchside-stories/02-story-seesaw/c/screenshot.png)

### Direction 1: Two windows — A, B and C

**Finding:** Predetermined thought bubbles substituted adult assumptions for students’ perspective-taking.

**Revised task:** Explore possible perspectives when someone wants to join a game.

**Evidence to assess:** Student considers uncertain perspectives, compares two responses and rehearses a constructive invitation or request.

[A](../switchside-stories/01-two-windows/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../switchside-stories/01-two-windows/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../switchside-stories/01-two-windows/c/screenshot.png)

**Transfer check:** Rehearse a similar situation in a supported role-play and discuss how the response affected each person.

## Instruction Impossible

Students write directions for a character that follows explicit steps. A test reveals missing information, ambiguous language or an incorrect sequence. Students use the visible result to revise their instructions, retest them and exchange challenges so a classmate can follow the final version.

**Primary outcome:** Write an ordered procedure with clear actions and reference points; use a failed test to revise it so another reader can complete the task.

**Lesson placement:** procedural writing, preparation for a science method or a connection between English and algorithmic thinking.

### Direction 3: Paper pilot — A, B and C

**Finding:** The suggested correction duplicated a later bridge-crossing step and conflicted with the map.

**Revised task:** Find and revise an ambiguous navigation step.

**Evidence to assess:** Student adds the missing direction or landmark, retests the complete sequence and checks a partner can follow it.

[A](../instruction-impossible/03-paper-pilot/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../instruction-impossible/03-paper-pilot/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../instruction-impossible/03-paper-pilot/c/screenshot.png)

### Direction 5: Direction workshop — A, B and C

**Finding:** The task lacked a defined starting orientation and a complete testable instruction sequence.

**Revised task:** Specify actions, direction and target so a literal reader can complete a procedure.

**Evidence to assess:** Student resolves ambiguity in a procedure, tests the complete sequence and names what changed.

[A](../instruction-impossible/05-direction-workshop/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../instruction-impossible/05-direction-workshop/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../instruction-impossible/05-direction-workshop/c/screenshot.png)

**Transfer check:** Give a partner a new physical or paper-based task using only written instructions, then revise from their feedback.

## Edit Echidna

Teachers assign one revision goal, such as clearer sequencing or stronger supporting detail. Students revise a short passage from their own writing, compare the versions and explain how a specific change helps the reader. The original meaning and the student’s authorship remain central.

**Primary outcome:** Make a focused change that meets a stated writing goal, preserves intended meaning and can be explained in terms of its effect on the reader.

**Lesson placement:** the revision stage of a writing workshop, immediately after feedback or in a short teacher conference.

### Direction 1: Small steps studio — A, B and C

**Finding:** The model revision reversed events without establishing the author’s intended meaning.

**Revised task:** Revise event order to match the author’s intended sequence.

**Evidence to assess:** Student restores the planned sequence, adds a useful link and explains how the change helps a reader.

[A](../edit-echidna/01-small-steps-studio/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../edit-echidna/01-small-steps-studio/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../edit-echidna/01-small-steps-studio/c/screenshot.png)

### Direction 3: Before and better — A, B and C

**Finding:** The polished supplied version changed the action and made the student’s revision decision invisible.

**Revised task:** Add a relevant detail and explain its effect on the reader.

**Evidence to assess:** Student adds a detail that clarifies an existing event and explains its purpose while retaining meaning.

[A](../edit-echidna/03-before-and-better/a/screenshot.png) · [B](../edit-echidna/03-before-and-better/b/screenshot.png) · [C](../edit-echidna/03-before-and-better/c/screenshot.png)

**Transfer check:** Apply the same revision goal to a new piece of the student’s writing without sentence-level suggestions.
