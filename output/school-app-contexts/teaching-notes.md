# Teaching notes for the additional app contexts

24 retained screenshots across 12 selected designs and 12 apps. Original screen numbers are preserved. Each screen includes an outcome, assessment evidence and lesson use.

Each screenshot is a static design concept. The controls show proposed app behaviour. Outcomes describe what to assess, not proven learning effects. Use the task after relevant teaching, record prompts and support, and check transfer to an unfamiliar example. Context numbers identify tasks; they are not a fixed year-level progression.

For Classroom Words, introduce every displayed sound–letter relationship first. The article “a” is assumed to be a previously taught familiar word. Capital letters represent the same taught correspondences. Oral directions can be teacher-supported. For other apps, adjust reading load and scaffolding without supplying the reasoning being assessed.

## Classroom Words · 5B · Sound buttons

Selected direction: Sound circles. Treatment: Tactile studio.

### 1. New vowel, new word

**Outcome:** Blend a CVC word with a newly taught vowel and read it in a controlled sentence.

**Evidence:** Listen for /d/ /o/ /g/ blended as dog and accurate reading of the sentence; note audio support.

**Lesson use:** After explicit teaching of short o; transfer to dot or got on paper.

[Open screenshot](classroom-words/05-sound-circles/b/01/screenshot.png)

### 2. Two letters, one sound

**Outcome:** Read sh as one sound and blend it with other taught correspondences.

**Evidence:** Student blends shop without separating s and h, then reads Sam sat in a shop.; check a different sh word.

**Lesson use:** After teaching sh; use ship as the next independent word.

[Open screenshot](classroom-words/05-sound-circles/b/02/screenshot.png)

## Think in Parts · 4A · Ink canvas

Selected direction: Voice and ink. Treatment: Quiet clarity.

### 1. The shrinking puddle

**Outcome:** Explain evaporation and predict how shade could affect a puddle under otherwise similar conditions.

**Evidence:** Diagram and narration link liquid water to water vapour in air; prediction has a reason and recognises other conditions.

**Lesson use:** After observing puddles or modelling changes of state; discuss what the diagram cannot show.

[Open screenshot](think-in-parts/04-voice-and-ink/a/01/screenshot.png)

### 2. Change the pivot

**Outcome:** Use a lever model to explain how pivot position affects the effort required for the same load.

**Evidence:** Student relates distances from the pivot to the load and effort and provides a consistent diagram/narration.

**Lesson use:** Consolidation after a physical lever demonstration; predict before testing.

[Open screenshot](think-in-parts/04-voice-and-ink/a/02/screenshot.png)

Selected direction: People’s assembly. Treatment: Quiet clarity.

**Outcome:** Recommend a council project within a budget while addressing competing access and comfort needs.

## Wild Notebook · 1C · Field record

Selected direction: Field journal. Treatment: Graphic play.

### 1. Weather at one spot

**Outcome:** Record a temperature comparison with consistent method and separate measured change from explanation.

**Evidence:** Student uses units, time and location, identifies a measured difference and proposes a further observation.

**Lesson use:** Outdoor weather mission followed by a short classroom comparison.

[Open screenshot](wild-notebook/01-field-journal/c/01/screenshot.png)

### 2. A puddle after rain

**Outcome:** Use repeated observations of one location to describe change while recognising limits of the measurement.

**Evidence:** Student distinguishes a shorter puddle from a measured volume loss and proposes a way to improve evidence.

**Lesson use:** After rainfall observation; revisit safely with teacher and compare class records.

[Open screenshot](wild-notebook/01-field-journal/c/02/screenshot.png)
