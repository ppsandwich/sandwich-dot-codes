Classroom Words

Foundation–Year 2 / Literacy

Teachers choose the sounds already taught. Children practise blending, then read short decodable sentences using those sounds.

Why I’m making it

01 / 12

The learning goal

Read an unfamiliar word independently using taught sound–letter relationships.

Use it for

A short literacy rotation, with the same sound set available for practice at home.

Who it’s for

Early years teachers · parents

Similar options

Fast Phonics ↗

Why choose this?

The proposed draw: teachers set the sequence, so practice stays in step with this week’s teaching. Parents get a clear starting point.

  2. 028 min: blend and read
  3. 03Read a new word on paper

Think in Parts

Years 5–9 / Cross-curricular explanation

Students combine drawing, movement and voice to explain how something works. Teachers set a challenge and review the explanation.

Why I’m making it

I find Explain Everything too expensive. I want to offer teachers and schools a more affordable way to create and assess explanations.

02 / 12

The learning goal

Explain cause and effect, then predict what happens when one part changes.

Use it for

A quick check of understanding in science, maths or humanities.

Who it’s for

Upper primary · secondary teachers

Similar options

Explain Everything already combines whiteboarding and recorded explanations.

Explain Everything ↗

Why choose this?

A focused explanation task could make reasoning easier to assess. The opportunity is useful prompts and review, in a well-served creation category.

In a lesson

  1. 01Model a process
  2. 0212 min: draw and explain
  3. 03Partner tests a prediction

Town Together

Students weigh community proposals, hear different views and work within a budget in a simulated council or parliament.

Why I’m making it

I think civics is underserved by learning apps. There’s room for a substantial library of scenarios, issues and decisions that students can keep coming back to.

03 / 12

Use it for

A small-group council simulation inside a civics inquiry.

Who it’s for

Humanities teachers · school teams

Wild Notebook

Years 5–9 / Science

Students record biodiversity, weather and water observations, compare evidence and bring their findings back to class.

Why I’m making it

I feel we’re losing the field-notebook habit. I want to bring it into the digital age, getting students outside to observe, record and explore science in real places.

04 / 12

The learning goal

Make a comparison from consistent observations and explain what the evidence cannot establish.

Use it for

A field record for an outdoor science lesson or a longer local inquiry.

Who it’s for

Science teachers · school teams

Similar options

Seek identifies living things and offers observation challenges.

Seek by iNaturalist ↗

Why choose this?

The proposed focus is the whole investigation: a shared method, comparable records and a classroom explanation across several science topics.

In a lesson

  1. 01Agree a method
  2. 0220 min: observe outside
  3. 03Compare findings and limits

TinkerTest

Years 5–9 / Science

Students choose variables, predict outcomes and interpret virtual experiments when a physical investigation is impractical.

Why I’m making it

Physical experiments aren’t always practical or possible. I want to make them accessible through clear visual simulations, giving schools more ways to run meaningful science lessons.

05 / 12

Use it for

Who it’s for

Research / 11 October 2026

Where the opportunity sits.

These are product concepts. The comparisons are sourced; the proposed advantages and routes to customers still need testing with teachers and parents.

What comparable products offer

  • Fast Phonics ↗

    Describes sequenced lessons using systematic synthetic phonics, reinforcing classroom learning.

Proposed route to customers

Trial with early years teams; test a school licence with teacher-directed home practice.

What we still need to learn

Test whether sound-set setup is quick enough for weekly use, and whether children transfer the skill to unseen words.

What comparable products offer

  • Explain Everything ↗

    Students can record screencasts of their understanding and collaborate on digital whiteboards.

Proposed route to customers

Pilot one subject unit with a teaching team before testing a cross-subject school licence.

What we still need to learn

Compare teacher review time and the misconceptions found against a normal whiteboard recording task.

What comparable products offer

  • iCivics: Counties Work ↗

    Players run a county; learning objectives cover government responsibilities and the effects of budgets and revenue on services and citizens.

Proposed route to customers

Start with a civics unit pack; test repeat use and willingness to buy further scenarios.

What we still need to learn

Check local curriculum relevance, preparation time and whether students justify a trade-off rather than simply vote.

What comparable products offer

  • Seek by iNaturalist ↗

    Uses image recognition to identify organisms, with badges and observation challenges.

Proposed route to customers

Pilot a school-ground science unit; assess repeat use across weather, water and habitat topics.

What we still need to learn

Test whether students collect comparable data and whether teachers can manage the outdoor-to-classroom transition easily.

What comparable products offer

  • PhET ↗

    Provides simulations and classroom facilitation strategies for inquiry.

  • ExploreLearning Gizmos ↗

    Describes inquiry-based virtual labs where students can repeat experiments and investigate concepts.

Proposed route to customers

Pilot a small investigation set with science departments before expanding the simulation library.

What we still need to learn

Compare preparation and assessment effort with a PhET or Gizmos lesson; check transfer to a physical experiment.

What comparable products offer

  • Osmo Pizza Co. ↗

    Combines tangible play pieces with a pizza business game covering addition, subtraction and fractions.

Proposed route to customers

Trial with primary maths teams; separately test parent interest in optional home challenges.

What we still need to learn

Test whether students explain the operation and apply it to a different recipe without prompts.

What comparable products offer

  • Book Creator ↗

    Provides speaking and listening templates, including picture description and voice recording.

Proposed route to customers

Pilot in early years and EAL/D groups, then test a class licence around repeat recording routines.

What we still need to learn

Compare clarity between tellings and teacher review effort; test use with multilingual learners without judging accent as ability.

What comparable products offer

  • National Geographic MapMaker ↗

    Offers curated maps and activities, 2D/3D exploration, sketching and custom maps for students.

Proposed route to customers

Begin with lower-primary map skills; validate advanced scale tasks separately before serving the full year range.

What we still need to learn

Test independent map use across the wide year range; compare with paper maps and existing mapping tools.

What comparable products offer

  • ReadTheory ↗

    Describes adaptive reading comprehension that matches texts and questions to each student’s reading level.

Proposed route to customers

Pilot one reading group, then test a school literacy licence if teachers use the evidence to plan follow-up teaching.

What we still need to learn

Check whether explanations reveal misconceptions and whether the review workload is manageable in a normal reading group.

What comparable products offer

  • Everyday Speech ↗

    Offers illustrated school scenarios with prompts for discussing feelings and perspectives in elementary settings.

Proposed route to customers

Pilot through classroom and wellbeing teams; assess repeat use without positioning it as therapy or a behaviour score.

What we still need to learn

Check that children can explain both perspectives; co-design with diverse learners and test teacher confidence facilitating discussion.

What comparable products offer

  • Code.org: Maze sequence ↗

    Students order movement commands as sequential steps in a program using a block-based maze activity.

Proposed route to customers

Start with tightly scoped procedural-writing challenges and teacher trials before expanding the language the character understands.

What we still need to learn

Test whether interpretation is predictable and feedback is fair; check transfer to real written instructions.

What comparable products offer

  • Quill ↗

    Provides supplemental writing exercises in short sessions, with feedback and opportunities to revise sentences.

Proposed route to customers

Trial during an existing writing unit; test a team licence if it becomes part of the regular feedback routine.

What we still need to learn

Check independent use of the revision move in later writing and whether teachers spend less time repeating the same feedback.

What every school pilot needs to check

Teacher preparation and review time, repeat use, willingness to pay and learning transfer. Also: accessible tasks, private classroom access, minimal data collection, deletion controls, device access and adult supervision. Outdoor work needs school safety procedures. A teacher trial doesn’t establish school or system purchasing approval.

The images show proposed behaviour. They don’t demonstrate working products or learning results. Lesson timings are suggestions.
